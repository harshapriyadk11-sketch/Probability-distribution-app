Introduction to Discrete Random Variables

In probability theory and machine learning, a discrete random variable takes on a finite or countably infinite set of distinct values. The probability distribution function assigns a probability $P(X = k)$ to each outcome such that the sum of all probabilities over the support equals 1.

Use this interactive dashboard to modify model parameters, evaluate probability mass functions (PMF) and cumulative distribution functions (CDF), visually interpret analytical plots with mean indicators, and complete structured laboratory exercises.

Fixed Trials

Rate / Interval

Poisson Distribution

Models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space at a constant average rate $\lambda$.

Trial to Success

Geometric Distribution

Models the number of independent trials required to achieve the very first success with success probability $p$.

Geometric Distribution

The Geometric distribution models the number of independent trials $X \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$ required to obtain the first success, where each trial succeeds with constant probability $p$.

Geometric Distribution Insights

  • Higher values of $p$ make the first success more likely to occur on early trials.
  • Lower values of $p$ increase the expected waiting time ($E[X] = 1/p$).
  • The distribution is strictly right-skewed for any $0 < p < 1$ with a mode always at $k = 1$.
  • It possesses the memoryless property: $P(X > s + t \mid X > s) = P(X > t)$.
